Annual preventive maintenance is the most thorough and technically demanding service in the HVAC maintenance calendar. It encompasses everything covered in your monthly routines, plus deep mechanical overhauls, component replacements, pressure testing, and full compliance documentation. Many annual tasks — particularly work on high-pressure refrigerant circuits, pressure vessels, and electrical systems — require certified specialist contractors. Plan the annual service well in advance to arrange access, specialist labour, and any necessary shutdowns.

<Warning>
  In many jurisdictions, annual service records for refrigerant-containing equipment, pressure vessels, electrical installations, and fire safety systems are **legally required**. Failure to maintain complete and up-to-date records can result in regulatory penalties, invalidated insurance, and health and safety liability. Ensure all work is carried out by appropriately qualified and certified engineers.
</Warning>

<Note>
  This checklist provides a structured task reference. For step-by-step procedures, torque specifications, refrigerant charge data, and equipment-specific guidance, refer to the individual equipment pages in this documentation. Always cross-reference the original manufacturer's service manual for your specific model.
</Note>

<AccordionGroup>

  <Accordion title="Chiller System Annual Service">

    - [ ] Perform full refrigerant circuit leak test using calibrated electronic detector; log result and refrigerant quantity checked in F-gas register
    - [ ] Take and send compressor oil sample for laboratory analysis (acid number, moisture content, metal particle count); replace oil if analysis indicates contamination
    - [ ] Inspect and clean chiller evaporator tube bundle — carry out tube-side eddy current test to detect wall thinning or pitting; record results
    - [ ] Inspect and clean condenser tube bundle (water-cooled units) — brush clean, hydrojet if scale is present, and test for leaks post-cleaning
    - [ ] Inspect and replace elastomeric refrigerant circuit seals (O-rings, gaskets) on all service valves and access fittings
    - [ ] Overhaul chilled water and condenser water pump mechanical seals; replace if leaking or showing wear; check impeller clearance
    - [ ] Full electrical inspection: measure insulation resistance (IR) of all motors, heaters, and control circuits; record MΩ values and compare to previous year
    - [ ] Calibrate all pressure and temperature sensors and transducers against certified reference instruments; record as-found and as-left values
    - [ ] Test and verify all safety cut-out set points: high-pressure, low-pressure, oil differential, freeze protection, and motor overloads — simulate faults where possible
    - [ ] Inspect chiller control panel: check all contactors for contact wear, replace if pitting depth exceeds 1 mm; check relay coil voltages
    - [ ] Check and re-torque all electrical power connections (bus bars, cable lugs, terminal blocks) to manufacturer specification
    - [ ] Review and update chiller performance baseline data (leaving/entering water temps, kW/TR, compressor amps) at standard test conditions
    - [ ] Inspect all pipe supports, expansion joints, and flexible connections in the chilled and condenser water circuits; replace deteriorated components
    - [ ] Complete F-gas leak check log entry with refrigerant type, quantity, GWP, and certifying engineer details
    - [ ] Issue annual chiller service report including findings, corrective actions, and recommendations for the next service period

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="AHU Annual Service">

    - [ ] Replace all filter banks (pre-filter, secondary filter, and HEPA/terminal filters where fitted); record filter class, brand, and replacement date
    - [ ] Deep-clean cooling and heating coils using biocide-grade coil cleaner and low-pressure rinse; inspect for corrosion and repair as needed
    - [ ] Disinfect and deep-clean condensate drain pan and pipework with approved biocide; swab test for Legionella and biological contamination if required by risk assessment
    - [ ] Inspect fan impellers for wear, erosion, or imbalance — carry out dynamic balancing if vibration levels exceed 2.8 mm/s RMS
    - [ ] Replace fan drive belts regardless of apparent condition; record belt type, cross-section, and quantity fitted
    - [ ] Check and re-grease all fan and motor bearings; measure bearing temperature under load and compare to previous year
    - [ ] Test all motorised dampers through full operational range; check actuator torque, blade sealing, and linkage condition; lubricate pivot pins
    - [ ] Inspect AHU casing panels and access doors: check seals, latches, and panel condition; repair or replace missing insulation sections
    - [ ] Full electrical check on AHU panel: test motor starters, overloads, and control circuit fuses; measure motor winding resistance and insulation resistance
    - [ ] Calibrate all BMS-connected sensors (temperature, humidity, CO₂, differential pressure); document as-found and as-left calibration values
    - [ ] Review and update AHU commissioning data — measure actual supply and return air volumes and compare to design specification; adjust belt drive or VFD trim if needed
    - [ ] Inspect humidifier (if fitted) — descale electrode or steam cylinder, clean distribution manifold, and test hygiene pack replacement cycle

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="DX Split AC Annual Service">

    - [ ] Deep-clean indoor unit evaporator coil using pump-up pressure spray and approved coil cleaner; protect electrical components during cleaning
    - [ ] Disinfect indoor unit casing, blower wheel, and drain tray with biocide solution; check for biological growth
    - [ ] Inspect and flush condensate drain system — clear any partial blockages, treat pipework with biocide tablet
    - [ ] Perform refrigerant leak check on full system: indoor unit connections, outdoor unit connections, line set pipe joints, and service port caps
    - [ ] Record operating pressures and temperatures; calculate superheat and sub-cooling; compare to manufacturer's published values for the refrigerant type and ambient conditions
    - [ ] Measure and record compressor running amps; compare to rated FLA on nameplate
    - [ ] Inspect outdoor unit condenser coil — deep-clean fins with pump spray; check for corrosion (especially coastal or industrial sites); apply anti-corrosion fin coating if recommended
    - [ ] Check outdoor unit fan motor: measure insulation resistance (target > 1 MΩ to earth); check for shaft seal leakage on sealed bearing units
    - [ ] Inspect interconnecting refrigerant pipework insulation — replace any sections where insulation is cracked, compressed, or missing
    - [ ] Check all electrical connections at indoor and outdoor units: tighten terminals, inspect contactors, test capacitor microfarad value against rating
    - [ ] Test all operating modes (cooling, heating, fan-only, dehumidification) and confirm each mode achieves set point within specification
    - [ ] Update system label on outdoor unit with last service date, refrigerant type, quantity, and certifying engineer details

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Water-Cooled Package AC Annual Service">

    - [ ] Replace or deep-clean supply air filter bank; record filter specification and replacement date
    - [ ] Deep-clean evaporator coil; inspect tube header plates for corrosion or scale pitting; pressure-test coil if visual condition is suspect
    - [ ] Inspect and clean condensate system — drain pan, trap, and pipe; apply biocide treatment
    - [ ] Overhaul condenser water circuit connections; clean water strainer and check basket integrity; inspect Y-strainer mesh size vs. system specification
    - [ ] Record entering and leaving condenser water temperatures under full load; calculate condenser approach temperature and compare to clean-coil baseline
    - [ ] Clean condenser tubes using rotary brush and hydraulic jet if scale or fouling is present; test for leaks after cleaning
    - [ ] Perform refrigerant leak check; check refrigerant charge using superheat and sub-cooling method; log in F-gas register
    - [ ] Inspect compressor oil — take sample for lab analysis if equipment is > 5 years old or has experienced a major repair
    - [ ] Full electrical inspection: insulation resistance tests on all motors; contact inspection on all contactors and starters; tighten all power terminals
    - [ ] Test all safety devices: high-pressure switch, low-pressure switch, flow switches (chilled and condenser water), and motor overloads
    - [ ] Calibrate unit controller sensors and verify set points match site operating requirements; document parameter schedule
    - [ ] Issue annual service report with performance data, safety test results, and outstanding recommendations

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Air-Cooled Package AC Annual Service">

    - [ ] Replace or deep-clean supply air filter bank; record filter specification and replacement date
    - [ ] Deep-clean evaporator coil using approved coil cleaner; inspect for corrosion and damage
    - [ ] Inspect and clean condensate drain pan and trap; test drainage with pour test; apply biocide treatment
    - [ ] Deep-clean air-cooled condenser coil(s) — blow fins from inside out with low-pressure compressed air; use coil cleaner for stubborn fouling
    - [ ] Straighten damaged condenser fins using appropriate fin comb; aim to restore > 90% fin contact area
    - [ ] Inspect all condenser fan motors: measure insulation resistance; check blade pitch, tip clearance, and blade hub security
    - [ ] Perform full refrigerant leak check; calculate superheat and sub-cooling; correct refrigerant charge if required; update F-gas log
    - [ ] Check compressor oil sight glass — record oil level and colour; replace if oil is dark or acid test indicates contamination
    - [ ] Full electrical inspection: insulation resistance tests on compressor motors and fan motors; check contactors, capacitors, and control fuses
    - [ ] Test all safety cut-outs: high-pressure, low-pressure, and motor overloads; verify set points against manufacturer's data sheet
    - [ ] Inspect refrigerant pipework insulation on suction lines; replace deteriorated sections
    - [ ] Apply anti-corrosion treatment to condenser coil and cabinet if site is coastal, industrial, or high-humidity environment

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Refrigeration Plant Annual Service">

    - [ ] Complete full F-gas leak check on every circuit; document refrigerant type, system charge weight, annual leak rate %, and certifying engineer — mandatory for systems ≥ 5 tonne CO₂e
    - [ ] Replace refrigerant filter-drier cores; test moisture content with moisture indicator or sight glass colour; log replacement in service record
    - [ ] Inspect and overhaul TXV or EEV: clean strainer screen, check valve operation, reset superheat to design value
    - [ ] Inspect and replace compressor suction and discharge service valve stem O-rings and cap seals where leakage is detected
    - [ ] Change compressor crankcase oil if lab analysis or manufacturer schedule requires; use correct grade and viscosity for refrigerant type
    - [ ] Inspect oil separator (where fitted): drain and replace oil charge; inspect float valve operation; check heating element if electrically traced
    - [ ] Deep-clean condenser — air-cooled: fin cleaning and fan service; water-cooled: tube brushing and pressure test; evaporative: full basin clean and water treatment check
    - [ ] Inspect and clean evaporator(s): check for ice bridging, frost pattern anomalies, and evidence of liquid carry-over
    - [ ] Test defrost system fully: initiate manual defrost, record defrost duration, confirm termination by temperature or time, and verify drain heater operation
    - [ ] Test all safety devices: high-pressure cut-out (simulate fault), low-pressure cut-out, oil pressure differential switch, condenser fan overload, and high-temperature alarms
    - [ ] Carry out electrical inspection — insulation resistance on all motors; check contactors, overloads, and phase-sequence relay; tighten all power terminals
    - [ ] Calibrate all temperature controllers, pressure transducers, and thermostats; record as-found and as-left values
    - [ ] Inspect refrigerated cabinet or cold room: check door gaskets, door heaters, lighting, and internal structural condition
    - [ ] Update refrigerant charge label on plant with service date, certified engineer name, and current refrigerant quantity

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Condenser Annual Service">

    - [ ] Deep-clean air-cooled condenser coils — remove unit casing panels where necessary, wash fins from inside face outward with pump spray and coil cleaner
    - [ ] Carry out fin inspection and straightening across the full coil face; record estimated % of damaged fins before and after service
    - [ ] Inspect and test all condenser fan motors: megger test (target > 1 MΩ to earth), check bearing condition, measure no-load running amps
    - [ ] Replace condenser fan motor capacitors if measured microfarad value is outside ± 5% of nameplate rating
    - [ ] For water-cooled condensers: inspect water boxes and end covers for corrosion and scale; inspect sacrificial anodes and replace at < 50% remaining mass
    - [ ] For water-cooled condensers: carry out hydrostatic pressure test on waterside circuit at 1.5× maximum allowable working pressure (MAWP); log result
    - [ ] For evaporative condensers: deep-clean basin and sump; flush pipework; service drift eliminators; inspect spray nozzles and replace blocked nozzles
    - [ ] Check and test water treatment system (conductivity controller, dosing pumps, bleed valve) for evaporative and water-cooled condensers
    - [ ] Inspect condenser refrigerant headers and brazed joints for oil staining or leak indication; leak test all joints with electronic detector
    - [ ] Inspect condenser mounting frame and vibration isolation mounts; replace rubber isolators showing cracking, compression set, or oil contamination
    - [ ] Verify condenser approach temperature against design specification at recorded ambient conditions; document finding

  </Accordion>

  <Accordion title="Compressor Annual Service">

    - [ ] Change compressor crankcase oil per manufacturer's annual schedule or lab analysis result; dispose of used oil through licensed waste contractor
    - [ ] Inspect and replace compressor crankcase sight glass and O-ring if discolouration or crazing is present
    - [ ] Test crankcase heater: measure heater element resistance and compare to rated value; replace heater if resistance is open-circuit or > 20% outside rated value
    - [ ] Carry out megger test on compressor motor windings — record winding-to-winding and winding-to-earth insulation resistance; trend against previous years
    - [ ] Measure compressor motor winding resistance on all phases; confirm balance within 2% phase-to-phase
    - [ ] Inspect compressor discharge and suction valves (reciprocating types) — check valve plates, springs, and seats; replace valve assembly if efficiency loss or noise is detected
    - [ ] Check compressor unloader solenoids (where fitted) — test electrical operation and confirm capacity step changes function correctly
    - [ ] Inspect all refrigerant service valves on compressor: re-pack valve stems, replace cap O-rings, and confirm valve positions are correctly set and capped
    - [ ] Check and re-torque compressor mounting bolts and anti-vibration mounts; replace mounts showing cracking, oil soaking, or loss of height
    - [ ] Inspect compressor discharge muffler and flexible connections — check for cracks, erosion, or vibration fatigue at brazed ends
    - [ ] Record and compare annual performance data: suction/discharge pressures, running amps, oil pressure differential, and bearing temperatures — trend for deterioration

  </Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>

## Compliance & Documentation

Ensure all of the following compliance documents are current and filed before closing out the annual service. Retain records for a minimum period as required by local regulations — typically 5 years for refrigerant logs and electrical certificates.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="F-Gas Leak Check Log" icon="file-lines">
    Required for all systems containing fluorinated refrigerants. Must record system identifier, refrigerant type, charge weight, GWP, annual leak rate, check date, and the name and F-gas certification number of the engineer performing the check. Systems ≥ 5 tonne CO₂e require checks every 12 months; systems ≥ 50 tonne CO₂e every 6 months.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Pressure Vessel Inspection Certificate" icon="certificate">
    Pressure vessels (receivers, separators, shell-and-tube heat exchangers) must be inspected by a competent person in accordance with the Pressure Systems Safety Regulations (PSSR) or equivalent local legislation. The written scheme of examination and inspection report must be kept on site and available for inspection.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Legionella Risk Assessment" icon="droplets">
    Water systems associated with cooling towers, evaporative condensers, and humidifiers require a current Legionella risk assessment. Review and update the assessment annually, or sooner if the system changes. Ensure water treatment records, monthly microbiological test results, and cleaning certificates are filed alongside the risk assessment.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Fire Damper Test Certificate" icon="fire-flame-simple">
    Fire dampers within ductwork must be drop-tested at the interval specified by local fire safety regulations (commonly every 12 months for high-risk areas and every 24 months for standard areas). Record damper location, test date, result (pass/fail/remedial action), and the name of the competent person conducting the test.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Electrical Test Certificate (PAT / EICR)" icon="bolt">
    Portable Appliance Testing (PAT) must be carried out on portable electrical equipment at appropriate intervals. Fixed electrical installations require a periodic Electrical Installation Condition Report (EICR) at intervals specified by BS 7671 or local equivalent. Both documents must be issued by a competent, registered electrician and kept on file.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Refrigerant Handling Records" icon="snowflake">
    Any addition, removal, or recovery of refrigerant must be recorded, including the quantity in kg, the reason for addition or removal, the certifying engineer's name and F-gas qualification number, and the date. Recovered refrigerant must be transferred to a licensed reclaim facility; document the transfer note and keep on file.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>
